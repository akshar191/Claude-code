Load Paths

Give a block of material a load, a couple of supports, and permission to keep only two fifths of itself. Nobody specifies a truss, an arch, or a fillet — but minimising compliance under a volume constraint finds them, and finds them again at every mesh resolution. Compliance is the work done by the load, c = fᵀu — the inverse of stiffness. Lower is stiffer. The volume constraint stays active throughout, so every layout you see holds exactly the same amount of material; only its arrangement changes.

01What the optimiser is actually doing

Each element of the mesh carries a density between 0 and 1, and its stiffness is interpolated from that density by the modified SIMP law:

Ee(xe) = Emin + xep · (E0 − Emin)

The exponent p is the whole trick. At p = 1 stiffness is proportional to density and half-dense material is a perfectly good deal, so the optimiser happily returns a grey mush. At p = 3 half-dense material costs half the volume but buys only an eighth of the stiffness, so intermediate densities stop paying for themselves and the design separates into solid and void. Slide the penalty down to 1 and watch the structure dissolve.

Because minimum compliance is self-adjoint, the gradient needs no adjoint solve — it falls out of the displacement field already computed:

∂c/∂xe = −p · xep−1 · (E0 − Emin) · ueᵀ ke ue

That quantity is the strain energy stored in an element. Read plainly, the algorithm's entire policy is: put material where the material you already have is working hardest, and take it from where it is idle.

02One iteration of the loop

STEP 1

Solve

Assemble K(x) from the current densities and solve K u = f. In this page that is conjugate gradients, warm started from the previous iteration's displacements.

STEP 2

Differentiate

Evaluate the strain energy in every element to get the compliance gradient in closed form. No finite differences, no second solve.

STEP 3

Filter

Average the gradient over a neighbourhood of radius rmin. This imposes a minimum member size and is what stops the design from fragmenting into a checkerboard.

STEP 4

Update

Move each density along its gradient, clipped to a 0.2 move limit, with a Lagrange multiplier found by bisection so the volume constraint stays exactly satisfied.

Repeat until no density changes by more than 1%. Typically 40 to 150 iterations — the counter above shows the live count.

03Why the filter is not optional

Without regularisation this problem is ill-posed. The optimiser discovers that alternating solid and void elements are artificially stiff in the finite element space it is working in, and drives the design towards a checkerboard that no manufacturing process could produce and no finer mesh would reproduce.

The filter fixes that by imposing a length scale. The test that proves it worked is not the compliance value — the filter also constrains member size, which has a genuine stiffness cost — but mesh independence. Holding the filter radius fixed in physical units and refining the mesh sixteen-fold, the Python implementation reproduces the same structure and the same compliance to 1.1%:

Refining a mesh should refine the resolution of an answer, not change the answer. That is the property that makes an optimised layout worth manufacturing.

04The solver underneath

An optimiser is only as trustworthy as the analysis driving it, so the finite element solver was verified before it was ever used to optimise anything. The test case is the end-loaded cantilever of Timoshenko and Goodier — a complete solution of the elasticity equations, not a beam-theory approximation. The exact displacement field is imposed on the supported end and the exact parabolic shear traction on the loaded end, leaving discretisation error as the only error present.

Tip deflection converges to 0.046% of the exact value, at the O(h²) rate the bilinear element is expected to deliver — measured O(h¹·⁹⁶). The reference deflection is PL³/3EI + P(4+5ν)c²L/6EI; that second term is transverse shear, worth 4.1% of the total here and entirely absent from Euler–Bernoulli theory. A solver that merely reproduced beam theory would fail this test.

The browser optimiser on this page reproduces the Python result to five significant figures — compliance 90.381 against 90.380 for the 40 × 20 cantilever, in the same 43 iterations.
